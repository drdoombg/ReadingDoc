What each layer means

  • Secant through a and a+dx: its slope df/dx is a real ratio of two real nudges. The derivative is what that ratio approaches as dx shrinks. dx never becomes 0.
  • Tangent: the best constant approximation of the rate near a. "Instantaneous rate" is shorthand for this.
  • f′ plots the tangent slope at every input. f″ plots how fast that slope changes: positive where the curve bends up, negative where it bends down.
  • Rectangles approximate Σ f(x)·dx from L to a. Here dx plays two roles: it is a factor in each term and the spacing between samples. Below the axis counts as negative (signed area).
  • A(x) is the area from L up to x. Its slope at a equals the height of f at a, because a nudge dx adds a sliver ≈ f(a)·dx. That is the fundamental theorem.
  • Average = area ÷ width. On A(x) the same number is the slope of the chord from L to a: average height of f = average slope of its antiderivative.
  • Taylor Pₙ copies the value, slope, curvature and higher derivatives of f at the single point a, and turns that into an approximation around a.

Try this

  • Press Shrink dx → 0. The secant rotates into the tangent and the gap readout drains to zero.
  • Press Zoom into a four or five times. The curve straightens into its own tangent line. Local linearity is the whole reason derivatives work.
  • Pick x³ − 3x, turn on f′ and f″. Where f″ crosses zero the bending flips and f′ hits a turning point.
  • Pick t(8 − t) as a velocity. Set L = 0 and drag a to 8: the area is the distance travelled, 85.33 m. Narrow the rectangles and watch Σ close in on ∫.
  • Pick eˣ and turn on f′, f″: all three curves sit on top of each other.
  • Pick ln x, put a at 1, Taylor order 10. It hugs ln x between 0 and 2 and then thrashes past 2: radius of convergence.
  • Pick sin x, set L = 0, a = π. The area is exactly 2 and the average is 2/π ≈ 0.64.

Chain rule · implicit differentiation · related rates · d(ln x)

Nudges that pass along

A nudge to one quantity forces nudges in everything tied to it. The chain rule follows a nudge down a pipeline. Implicit differentiation asks which nudges keep an equation true.
